## PR review: bookcraft: a reader test and revision loop held to a book-level budget

Reviewed: the uncommitted working tree against `feature/127-bookcraft-research-a-book-s-subject-and-cite-trusted-sources-with-verified-urls` (stacked base). Ticket: cjus/cjus-skills issue 129. No prior `pr-review-*.md` in this plan folder, so this is a first review.

### Summary

The PR adds step 8b to `/createbook`: a fresh agent reads the finished chapters as the persona, writes notes, and answers questions written from the coverage ledger. Its notes become a revision brief, revisers work in groups that follow chapter dependencies and add nothing net, and a second reader that never saw the first one's notes confirms the result. A new `reading_budget` key in `book.json` is validated by `check-book.sh`, which prints a `read time:` line for a primer or any book with a budget and reports when the book runs past it. `updatebook` carries the line into its report and suggests a re-test after a large primer edit.

### What is working well

- **The budget check is the right size.** It reports and never fails, so it matches the skill's standing rule that "time is a target and never a gate", and its rate (175 words a minute over `prose + structure`) is exactly what § The read-time estimate's step 9 row says. Rounding to nearest (`+ 87`) agrees with the "about" wording used everywhere else.
- **The report is counted.** It goes through `report()`, so `reports: 3` on the primer fixture includes it, and the manifest row asserts that count. A budget overrun cannot be hidden behind a passing run.
- **The validation follows the existing declaration pattern.** `book_model.py` hands the value over as `tostring`, and `check-book.sh` validates it in the same block as `tags`, `provenance` and `overview`. Probed by hand: `90.0`, `1e2`, `null`, `false`, `-5` and `0` all stop the run with exit 2 and an error naming the key, and `""` reads as absent.
- **`reader/` really is invisible.** I put notes containing an em dash and a block quote into `reader/` beside the primer fixture. `check-book.sh` and `check-provenance.sh` both ignored them, because `book_model.py` lists the folder one level deep (`folder.iterdir()`, `is_file()`).
- **The fixtures test what they claim.** `fixtures/primer` declares 3 minutes against about 6 (1,048 prose plus 78 structure is 1,126 words, which is 6.4 minutes) and asserts the line, the report and the count. `fixtures/research` asserts `budget: none declared` and the absence of the report, which covers the primer-without-budget branch.
- **NOTES.md explains why each part has the shape it has**: the brief before the revisers, the zero-net rule, the unanchored second reader, the rating left ungated. It does that without naming the trial's source material.
- `test-fixtures.sh --strict`: 26 passed, 0 failed, 0 skipped. No em dash, no attribution and no banned term in any added line.

#### Critical

#### Important

🟡 **What happens after the second reader is undefined, and "two rounds" reads two ways**

📍 Location: `plugins/bookcraft/skills/createbook/reference/reader-test.md:L294-L306`, `SKILL.md § 8b` (last bullet), `NOTES.md § The reader test` ("Why two rounds")

**What I see:**
"Two rounds at most. If the second reader still finds a blocking problem, report it to the operator with both readers' notes rather than running a third round. A problem that survives two revisions is usually in the outline."

**The risk:**
An agent cannot tell how many revision passes to run.
- If a round is one reading, the second reader's blocking finding goes straight to the operator after a single revision, and "survives two revisions" never happens.
- If a round is a reading plus a revision, round 2 revises on the second reader's notes and nobody confirms that revision.

Two cases are not covered at all:
- **The second reader finds real but non-blocking problems.** The trial's own headline finding, two factual errors, is not one of the three stop conditions, so read literally the loop stops and leaves them in the book.
- **The book is over budget but no reader finding blocks.** Being past the budget is not a reader finding, so the "report it to the operator" clause does not reach it.

`<N>` in `reading-notes-<N>.md` and `revision-brief-<N>.md` is never defined either.

**Suggested fix:**
Spell the sequence out in one short numbered list. For example:
1. Reader 1 writes `reading-notes-1`, which becomes `revision-brief-1` and a revision.
2. Reader 2 writes `reading-notes-2`.
3. If the stop conditions hold, fix any factual error or contradiction reader 2 named, with no new reader, and stop.
4. Otherwise write `revision-brief-2`, revise once more, and run reader 3 to confirm. Whatever reader 3 still finds goes to the operator.

Then say `<N>` is the reader's number. Adjust it to whichever loop you actually intend; the point is that the text names one.

**Learning note:**
A procedure an agent follows has to settle every branch it can reach. An agent that has to guess fills the gap with whichever reading is cheapest.

🟡 **The optional handbook path depends on a budget and a read-time line that a handbook book may not have**

📍 Location: `SKILL.md § 8b` ("Under the handbook profiles the step is optional"), `reference/reader-test.md:L281` (the brief's budget bullet), `L292`, `L302`

**What I see:**
The brief requires "the book's reading time now, from `check-book.sh`, against its `reading_budget`, and how many words each chapter may net out at". The stop condition is "the book is within its reading budget". But `check-book.sh` prints the `read time:` line only for a primer or a book with a budget, and nothing tells a handbook book to declare one. The § 4 handbook templates have no `reading_budget`. The brief's per-chapter word allowance handed to a reviser also runs against § Sizing the book ("never a budget handed to a chapter agent") and § The read-time estimate ("not a number to put in a chapter agent's prompt"). Those rules govern the handbook profiles, where `chapter-prose.md` sets no ceiling.

**The risk:**
An agent running the optional test on a guide or narration book hits a required input that does not exist and a stop condition it cannot evaluate. The per-chapter allowance also contradicts a rule the skill marks as a deliberate change.

**Suggested fix:**
Add one sentence to reader-test.md: "Where the book declares no `reading_budget`, the budget bullet and the budget stop condition do not apply. The zero-net rule still does, and the brief gives no per-chapter word figure under the handbook profiles." Alternatively, have § 8b say a handbook book tested on a reader declares a `reading_budget` first.

**Learning note:**
When a step is made optional for more cases, check each of its inputs against what those cases actually produce.

🟡 **updatebook suggests a reader test "on the chapters it reached", which the procedure cannot run**

📍 Location: `plugins/bookcraft/skills/updatebook/SKILL.md:L168`

**What I see:**
"...is worth `createbook/SKILL.md § 8b`'s reader test on the chapters it reached."

**The risk:**
reader-test.md's reader reads the finished chapters start to finish, and counts any term the book has not explained as unknown. A reader handed chapters 5 and 6 alone flags every term chapters 1 to 4 taught as a stumble. The questions also come from the whole coverage ledger, so many cannot be answered from a subset. The re-test would produce false findings, and a reviser acting on them would re-explain material the book already teaches, which the budget then punishes.

**Suggested fix:**
"...is worth § 8b's reader test on the whole book, with the questions limited to the coverage items those chapters teach." A cheaper alternative is to have the reader read from chapter 1 through the last chapter the edit reached.

**Learning note:**
The reader test is valid only because the reader has nothing but the book. Feed it a partial book and its findings lose that grounding.

🟡 **"Read time reported before and after" reaches the operator only as "after"**

📍 Location: `SKILL.md § 9. Report` (line 762); PLAN.md, "A budget revision is held to", item 1

**What I see:**
The before figure is written into `revision-brief-<N>.md`, and step 9 reports the final read time against the budget. Step 9's list of what the reader test reports covers questions answered, the rating and anything left for the operator, and leaves out the read time before revision.

**The risk:**
The objective's item says the read time is reported before and after. The drift the trial measured, 70 to 99 minutes, is visible only as a change, and the operator never sees the change unless they open the brief.

**Suggested fix:**
In step 9's reader-test clause, add "the read time before the first revision and after the last".

**Learning note:**
A budget's figure means most beside its trend, so put both numbers where the operator reads.

#### Suggestions

🟢 **Validation lets a leading-zero string through, so `"00"` becomes a budget of 0.** `check-book.sh:L320-L327`. The pattern `*[!0-9]*|0` rejects `0` but accepts the string `"00"`, which reports every book as past a budget of 00. It also accepts `"090"` and `"08"`, which print as typed. A 20-digit value passes the pattern and then prints `[: integer expression expected` at L1562 while the run carries on. JSON numbers never carry a leading zero, so `*[!0-9]*|0*|??????*)` rejects every leading zero and anything of six digits or more, at no cost to a real budget.

🟢 **The budget report prints inside the summary block.** `check-book.sh:L1558-L1564`. Every other `REPORT` line prints above the blank line that opens the summary. This one sits between `read time:` and `chapter prose:`, and updatebook describes the summary block line by line. It is counted correctly. Since `total_words` and `total_struct` are final by then, the comparison and its `report` could move above the `echo` at L1541, with only the `read time:` line kept below.

🟢 **No fixture covers the new rejection path.** That matches the existing declarations, none of which has an exit-2 row, so this is optional. A `mutated(...)` probe in `fixtures/research/run.sh` setting `reading_budget` to `"ninety"` and `"00"` would cover it, as the PR summary says was done by hand.

🟢 **The zero-net rule has no answer for a chapter with nothing marked "too much", or for a book well under budget.** `reader-test.md:L289`. Add a line: where the reader marked nothing in that chapter, cut the weakest passage, or say so in the findings file. Say whether the rule binds a book with room in its budget, too. As written it does, which is defensible, so say it on purpose.

🟢 **Step 9's sentence now nests a colon list inside a comma list.** "...the reader test's outcome where it ran: how many questions each reader answered, its rating, and anything left for the operator, how many chapters are mostly filled in..." reads as though the chapter count belongs to the reader test. A semicolon or parentheses around the reader-test clause would fix it.

🟢 **Revisers write to the same `outline-findings/<stem>.md` the drafting agent used**, which can overwrite the first-draft findings. Consider `outline-findings/<stem>-revision-<N>.md`.

🟢 **The step 3 gate never lists the reading budget among what it shows**, though § 4 says `reading_budget` is "the minutes the gate settled". Adding "and the reading budget, under primer" to the gate's show list closes the loop.

🟢 **The overview at `SKILL.md:L266`** ("derive the book, plan and confirm the outline, ... draft, check, report") could name the reader test now that a primer requires it.

#### ⏭️ Deferred to follow-up

Eight chapters for 90 minutes assumes about 2,000 words on the page per chapter, while the ceiling is 2,000 words of prose, so a book drafted near the ceiling runs past its budget by design.
