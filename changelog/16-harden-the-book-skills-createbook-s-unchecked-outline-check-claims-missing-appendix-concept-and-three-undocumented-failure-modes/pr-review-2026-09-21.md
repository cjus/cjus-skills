## Fixed in this branch

**A section citation ran through trailing prose.** `CITE_SECTION` terminated only on `,`, `;` and
`)`, so a cell reading `§ What The Grain Is — the definition the book opens on` was extracted whole
and failed as a heading the source does not carry. A correct ledger exited 1 at the step 3 gate,
blaming the source. Now also terminates on an en dash, an em dash and a pipe. **A colon is
deliberately not a terminator**, because a heading carries one often: the appendix fixture's own H1
is `# Appendix 1: The Reference Table`.

**A capitalised locator was counted and then asserted against nothing.** `CITE_PAGE` and
`CITE_SLIDE` carry `re.I`, because a ledger cell is prose and may open a sentence with
"Slides 4-9". `PAGES` and `SLIDES` are anchored lowercase and matched neither, so the locator
entered the census and then reported REVIEW as though it were the wrong kind for its source. The
run exited 0 on a check that had asserted nothing, which is the shape this whole family of scripts
refuses. `_lower_lead()` now folds the leading word only, so a heading keeps the case
`heading_hit` may compare on.

**A bad `--chapters` token tracebacked instead of exiting 2.** `parse_id_token` used
`str.isdigit()`, which is true for superscripts that `int()` then rejects, and the rewrite had
removed `main`'s `ValueError` guard. The script's contract separates exit 2 (usage) from exit 1
(failures found), so a caller read the traceback as "checks failed". Now `str.isdecimal()`.

**`APPENDIX_FILE` was unanchored, re-opening the collision it exists to close.** Graded a
suggestion by the reviewer; treated here as a defect, because
`guide-07-the-appendix-2-problem.md` returned `('appendix', 2)` — a chapter read as an appendix on
the strength of its slug. Now anchored and lowercase, matching `check-book.sh:291` exactly, and
that filename reads as chapter 7.

Also applied: `argument-hint` updated to `N,M,AN`; `tally()` emits "appendices" rather than
"appendixes"; `id_token()` was dead and now spells the tokens in the `--chapters` refusal; § 5 says
an absent `outline-findings/` folder is the all-clear.

Two suite assertions were passing for the wrong reason and were tightened: `NOT USED.*appendix`
matched the filename rather than the explanation, and the `--chapters B2` case asserted only the
exit code, which "no such folder" also produces.

Each fix carries a regression assertion. The two suites go from 26 assertions to 32.

## Accepted, not fixed

- `check_ledger` builds a fresh `Source` per row, so a PDF named by four rows is read four times.
  Real, but the ledger is tens of rows, not thousands. Memoizing is the right call if a ledger ever
  gets slow.
- A `Re-openable: No` row whose Source cell holds a URL ending `.pdf` draws a spurious REVIEW.
  Low risk: the ledger does not store URLs.
- `_tables` splits on raw `|`, so an escaped `\|` in a cell shifts every column right.
- `'0'` and `'A0'` parse and are caught downstream by the "has no ch. 0" refusal rather than at
  parse time.

## Confirmed sound

The `(kind, number)` refactor has no missed call site. `_id` is assigned immediately before every
`chapters.append(d)`, so no dict can reach `render()` without it, and it is never re-serialized
because the script writes markdown only. `order()` recomputes `file_id(d)` with a fallback, so no
sort can raise or compare `None`. The `want` map is keyed on the pair in both `main()` and
`load()`, and `sorted()` over `(str, int)` tuples is a total order.
