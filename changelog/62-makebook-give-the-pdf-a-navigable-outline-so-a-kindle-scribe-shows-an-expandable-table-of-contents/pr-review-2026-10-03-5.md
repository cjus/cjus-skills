## PR review: makebook: give the PDF a navigable outline, so a Kindle Scribe shows an expandable table of contents

Fifth close review of PR #63 (closes `#62 makebook-give-the-pdf-a-navigable-outline-so-a-kindle-scribe-shows-an-expandable-table-of-contents`).
This is a delta review of `eeb7026..ef3347d`, per the scope contract. The four earlier reviews'
conclusions stand and aren't re-derived here.

### Summary

The fourth review (NEEDS_DISCUSSION) found that the 17pt hyphen-wrap check in
`fixtures/bind/run.sh` required one realistic term to break at its hyphen. Only the macOS
runner's font metrics made it break, so the Linux leg went 17/1/0 even though the binder was
correct. `ef3347d` replaces that single term with the sweep the review proposed: eight entries,
"model" repeated n times and then "pre-training", for n from 1 to 8. The check asserts at least
one `pre-` / `training` wrap and all 16 links. It also adopts the review's optional suggestion,
a refusal for numbers that print against a term that doesn't match. Outside the fixture, only
the changelog folder changed. `build-book.py` is byte-identical to `eeb7026`.

### What is working well

- **A faithful, minimal application of the proposal.** The new `entries`, the filler pages, the
  `wraps` count over adjacent word pairs, the `words[-12:]` diagnostic and the `links=16`
  assertion all match the fourth review's sketch line for line. No other check in `run.sh`
  changed.
- **The wrap is still asserted before the links.** "wraps" is still read from line 1 of the
  probe's output, so a sweep that stopped wrapping on some future font fails loudly instead of
  passing without testing anything. That property was the best thing about the original check,
  and it survives the rewrite.
- **The sweep wraps on this machine, and I confirmed it isn't just luck.** I ran the committed
  probe against the current binder and got `wraps`, `links=16` and both refusals. On page 1 the
  hyphen breaks at n=4 and again at n=7. At n=3 the line breaks after "training", and at n=8
  "pre-training" opens a line. So the line end falls in different places across the sweep, and
  the steps are narrow enough that two of them land in the hyphen's window. That is the property
  the comment claims.
- **It still catches the regression it exists for.** The same probe against the `f7ba3da`
  binder, which anchored on the last word, raises `RuntimeError` on the seven-model entry. The
  check fails as it should.
- **The new mismatched refusal has teeth of its own.** The numbers `2, 3` print after every
  entry, so the run search always succeeds, and only the whole-term tail comparison can reject
  `"model post-training"`. That pins the comparison itself and not just the not-found path. As a
  mutation test, I compared the tail on the term's last 8 characters only. That looser binder
  still passes the wrap, `links=16` and the unprinted refusal, but prints
  `mismatched: accepted`, and the check then fails. So this case covers a regression nothing
  else in the suite would catch. The grep matches the full error text including the term's name,
  so another entry raising can't satisfy it.
- **Both legs are green on the head commit.** CI run 37147552667 on `ef3347d` reports success for
  `fixtures (macos-latest)` and `fixtures (ubuntu-latest)`. Each log shows
  `ok makebook/bind/run.sh` and `passed 18 failed 0 skipped 0`. Because "ok" requires the "wraps"
  line, the Linux runner's fonts did produce a hyphen wrap. That is the exact failure the fourth
  review raised, now resolved on the platform where it occurred.
- **The comment explains the design.** It records why a single term was fragile, why the step
  has to be narrower than the wrap window, and why the real stylesheet and `build_index()` are
  used. Someone tempted to "simplify" the sweep back to one term will see why not to.
