### Summary

`f7ba3da` fixes the second review's Important finding. `link_pages` used to match page
numbers one word at a time, so a bare number inside a term ("Top 2 lists") could take the
entry's first link. Now each entry is matched as a unit: a word whose punctuation-stripped,
casefolded text equals the term's **last word**, followed immediately by the entry's numbers
exactly as `build_index` prints them (`2,` `3`). The bind fails, naming the entry, when no
such run is found. A new check in `fixtures/bind/run.sh` renders "Top 2 lists  2, 3" and
"Week 4  4" through the binder's own `render()` and `link_pages()`. It asserts three links,
each over its own printed number, and no link over either term digit.

The fix is right for the case it targets. But anchoring on the last word adds a new failure
path: when the term's last word contains a hyphen and Chromium wraps the line at that hyphen,
the bind fails. I reproduced this with a realistic curated term at the large-print type size.
`main` binds the same input.

### What is working well

- **The new fixture is a real regression test, not a coverage number.** I ran its probe
  against `17283a1`'s `link_pages`, and it fails with both defects named: "the 2 in the term
  after 'Top' carries a link" and "the 4 in the term after 'Week' carries a link". Against
  `f7ba3da` it prints `made=3 links=3` / `clean`. The two-part assertion is well chosen. The
  count alone can't catch the bug, because the old matcher also made 3 links, so asserting
  where the links aren't is what gives the check its teeth.
- **The matcher is strict where it should be.** A run must read `2,` `3` exactly, so a
  number in prose or a heading can't stand in for one. A missing run now fails the bind
  instead of drifting back into step. That answers the second review's learning note
  directly.
- **The comparison is tolerant in the right places.** `html_mod.unescape` handles the
  `&amp;` that `pdftotext -bbox` writes. `\W+` stripping plus `casefold()` absorbs
  apostrophes, `++`, `&` and a trailing dash. `casefold()` also expands the `fi` and `fl`
  ligature code points (`'ﬁ'.casefold() == 'fi'`), so Palatino's ligatures can't break
  the comparison whichever way the ToUnicode map goes. Through the real stylesheet
  (`build_css()` plus `build_index()`), I checked "Python 3" `[3, 5]` (a digit as the last
  word), "C++", "Q & A", "file finder", "I/O" and "pause —". All of them linked correctly.
- **The structural assumptions hold.** `.index-entry` carries `break-inside: avoid`, so an
  entry never splits across a column or a page, and `pdftotext -raw -bbox` keeps an entry's
  words contiguous. Harvested terms are built from `TOKEN_RE` (`[A-Za-z0-9_]`), so a
  harvested display contains no hyphen, slash or bare number. **The default, harvested
  index can't hit the failure below.** Only a curated `terms.txt` can.
- **Building `placed` before touching the PDF** means a failed match raises before anything
  is written. The rewrite stays atomic (`tmp.replace(pdf)`).

#### Important

🟡 **A curated term whose last word wraps at its hyphen fails the bind**

📍 Location: `plugins/bookcraft/skills/makebook/scripts/build-book.py:2884-2892` (the
`last = bare(display.split()[-1])` anchor in `link_pages`), and the matching sentence in
`plugins/bookcraft/skills/makebook/SKILL.md:156`.

**What I see:**

```python
last = bare(display.split()[-1]) if display.split() else ""
for k in range(max(at, 1), len(words) - len(run) + 1):
    if (bare(words[k - 1][6]) == last
            and [w[6] for w in words[k:k + len(run)]] == run):
        break
else:
    raise RuntimeError(...)
```

The anchor is the term's last word as one whitespace-delimited token, compared against the
one PDF word just before the run. Chromium can break a line after a hyphen-minus (UAX #14
allows a break after `HY`). When it does, pdftotext reads the last word as two words,
`pre-` and `training`. The word before the run is then `training`, which never equals
`pretraining`, so the match misses.

**The risk:** I reproduced it with `build_css(MAX_BODY_PT)` and `build_index()`, then the
binder's `render()` and `link_pages()`, using the entry `large language model pre-training`
`[2, 3, 4, 5]`. At 17pt (the documented large-print size, within `--type-size`'s range), the
words are `... model pre-` / `training 2, 3, 4, 5`, and the bind raises:

```
the page numbers of 'large language model pre-training' (2,, 3,, 4,, 5) were not found after its term
```

`main()` turns that into `error: the index's page links in … are incomplete` and exits 1.
The PDF is left with its outline but no index links and no attached scripts. A sweep at
11.8pt raised for a hyphenated term of about 46 to 49 characters. At 17pt, a 33-character
term was enough. The window is narrow at any one size, because the hyphen has to be the last
break opportunity that fits. But it moves with `--type-size`, so a `terms.txt` that binds at
one size can fail at another. (Slashes didn't break in my tests: "client/server" passed at
every length.)

This passes the in-scope test: `main` has no `link_pages` and binds this input, and
`17283a1`'s number-only matcher would have bound it too. So it is a **regression against
`main` introduced by `f7ba3da`**. The input is documented: `SKILL.md`'s The index section
recommends curating `terms.txt`, and hyphenated terms are ordinary in technical books. There
is no good workaround. A non-breaking hyphen in `terms.txt` stops the derived pattern from
matching the prose, and `--no-index` gives up the index. The failure is loud, so no
mislinked book ships, which is why this isn't Critical.

**Suggested fix:** anchor on the **whole term**, compared as stripped text, instead of on one
token. Find the run first. Then rebuild the stripped text of the words just before it, back
toward `at`, until it is at least as long as the stripped term, and require that it ends with
the term. Stripping the hyphen makes `pre-` + `training` read as `pretraining`. A whole-term
comparison is also stricter than a last-word one, because it checks every word of the term.

```python
def bare(text: str) -> str:
    return re.sub(r"\W+", "", unicodedata.normalize("NFKC", text)).casefold()

placed, at = [], 0
for display, pgs in entries:
    run = [f"{p}," for p in pgs[:-1]] + [str(pgs[-1])]
    term = bare(display)
    for k in range(max(at, 1), len(words) - len(run) + 1):
        if [w[6] for w in words[k:k + len(run)]] != run:
            continue
        tail, j = "", k
        while j > at and len(tail) < len(term):
            j -= 1
            tail = bare(words[j][6]) + tail
        if tail.endswith(term):
            break
    else:
        raise RuntimeError(
            f"the page numbers of {display!r} ({', '.join(map(str, pgs))}) were "
            f"not found after its term")
    placed += zip(words[k:k + len(run)], pgs)
    at = k + len(run)
```

I tested exactly this on a scratch copy of the binder:

- the new `run.sh` probe still prints `made=3 links=3` / `clean`, so no term digit is linked;
- all five realistic hyphenated terms at 17pt link, including the failing one;
- the full 11.8pt sweep of hyphen and slash lengths links with no raise;
- every edge case listed under What is working well still links.

The `NFKC` step is cheap insurance now that every character of the term is compared: a
`terms.txt` saved in decomposed form would otherwise lose its accents to `\W` and miss
(`import unicodedata` is needed). Add a fixture case that pins the wrap, for example the
same probe style with `build_css(binder.MAX_BODY_PT)` and the entry above. It fails today,
and it doubles as the first test of the new `raise` path. Then update the `SKILL.md`
sentence and the docstring from "follows the last word of its term" to "follows its term".

**Learning note:** to match text against a PDF, compare it the way the PDF can disagree with
the source. Line breaking only removes whitespace and can split a word at a hyphen, so
"strip everything that isn't a letter or digit, then compare whole strings" survives it.
"Compare the last token" assumes token boundaries survive layout, and at a line end they
don't.
