#### Important

**1. § Placing slides puts a slide after the paragraph it supports, but the paragraph printed
beside it is the one after**

Location: `plugins/bookcraft/skills/updatebook/SKILL.md:277-278`, and the report at `:281`

**What I see:**
Bullet 1 says: "put the slide on a line of its own after the paragraph it supports". Bullet 2
says: "The paragraph after a slide prints beside it ... Where that paragraph does not speak to the
slide, rewrite it where it stands". The report then gives "the tag of the paragraph it follows".

**The risk:**
Bullet 1 carries the figure convention over to slides. A figure goes after the paragraph that
states what it shows (`makebook/SKILL.md:111`). But a slide row floats left
(`build-book.py:491-494`), so the art sits beside the *following* paragraph, and the fixture has
that shape: the slide, then `[1-6] The slide above ...`.

Here is what an agent following bullet 1 does:
1. It picks the paragraph P that the slide illustrates, and puts the slide after it.
2. The next paragraph, Q, is usually on the chapter's next point, so bullet 2 fires.
3. Q is rewritten into slide notes, although the chapter needs it for its own argument.

Had the slide gone directly before P, P would have printed beside it and nothing would have been
rewritten. Across a book's slides (66 in the one book measured), the current rule rewrites
paragraphs that never needed to change. A paragraph rewritten in place keeps its tag but changes
what a citation of that tag reaches. That is the failure `/updatebook` is built around
(§ Why in place rather than a rerun). Step 5's check 4 flags each one, so nothing is lost
silently, but every one costs a review.

**Suggested fix:**

```markdown
- **Place each slide where the chapter's lesson needs it, not where the old book had it.** [...]
  Read the chapter, find the paragraph that explains what the slide shows, and put the slide on a
  line of its own directly before that paragraph, after the previous unit's provenance mark and
  with a blank line on each side: `![Caption](<slide folder>/<file>)`. A slide row floats, so the
  paragraph after it prints beside the art and reads as its notes (`makebook/SKILL.md
  § Lesson-script slides`). That is the reverse of a figure, which follows the paragraph that
  states what it shows. The path has to start with [...]
- **Where no paragraph explains what the slide shows**, rewrite the one after it where it stands,
  and it keeps its tag, or add the explanation with a lettered tag (§ Adding prose).
```

The report line becomes "with its chapter and the tag of the paragraph beside it".

**Learning note:**
When a prompt borrows a convention from a neighbouring element, check it against what the
renderer does. A figure and a slide differ in exactly one respect here, which is where the text
that explains them sits.

**2. Lettered slide notes trip § Adding prose's "several at once" rule, and the rewrite that rule
calls for drops the slides**

Location: `plugins/bookcraft/skills/updatebook/SKILL.md:278`, against `:94`, `:197`, `:225` and
`:236-237`

**What I see:**
§ Placing slides says: "Where the slide needs notes no paragraph holds, add them with a lettered
tag (§ Adding prose)." Three existing rules point the other way:
- `:197` says "A chapter collecting several, or needing several at once, is due a rewrite".
- `:94` and `:225` make "needs several new paragraphs at once" the trigger for Rewrite one
  chapter.
- That rewrite drafts with createbook's step 5 prompt, which says nothing about figures or
  slides (`createbook/SKILL.md:475-511`), and then replaces the file whole (`:237`).

**The risk:**
A recreate carries none of the old book's slide notes. In the one book measured they were 8,733
words across 66 slides (`createbook/NOTES.md:511`), roughly four slides a chapter. A chapter given
three or four slides can plausibly need several notes paragraphs at once. The agent then faces
two rules that point opposite ways:
- § Placing slides says to letter the notes.
- § Adding prose says the chapter is due a rewrite.

If the agent follows the rewrite, the chapter file is replaced by a draft whose prompt places no
slide, and the slides it just placed are gone. Nothing reports that: by ruling, the bind does not
warn about unplaced slides, and the recreate's report has already been read.

**Suggested fix:**
Add one sentence to § Placing slides saying which rule wins. I recommend this one, because it
keeps the operator's ruling that drafting places no slide:

```markdown
Notes added for slides in a placing run do not count toward § Adding prose's several-at-once
rule: the chapter's claim has not moved, and a rewrite would drop the slides, because the chapter
prompt places none. `check-book.sh` still reports the lettered tags.
```

The inverse is also coherent: a chapter needing several is rewritten, with its slides handed to
the chapter agent. That is option 1 in `NOTES.md`, which the operator did not choose. See
Questions.

**Learning note:**
A new instruction inherits every threshold already in the skill it joins. Before shipping one,
read the rules its output feeds into, here the lettered-tag count.

#### Suggestions

**3. "Notes" is not defined against the guide profile's no-lesson-script rule.**
Location: `updatebook/SKILL.md:278`, and `createbook/NOTES.md:1296`.

`guide.md:193` says a teaching chapter "never scripts a lesson". It says words for a room, "such
as what to say over a slide", come from a companion document, and its Before example
(`guide.md:202`) is exactly a "What to say ... before slide 14" note. The new text asks for the
paragraph beside a slide to be the slide's "notes", and in a lesson-script book that is the form
the old notes took. Add a clause such as: "notes here means the paragraph explaining what the
slide shows, said to the reader, never what to say over it (`createbook/reference/guide.md § A
shape for a teaching chapter`)."

**4. The ordering sentence holds for tags but not for what they reach.**
Location: `createbook/SKILL.md:710`.

The sentence reads "Placing slides moves no tag, whether it happens before or after the outside
citations are repointed". That is true of tags. But a placing run can rewrite a paragraph in
place (`updatebook/SKILL.md:278`), which changes what a repointed citation of that tag now
reaches. updatebook step 5's check 4 catches each one, so this is about cost, not loss. Suggest
"prefer placing slides before repointing, so a paragraph rewritten as a slide's notes is
repointed once".

**5. "The placed count is what the bind's `slides:` line should then print" holds only for a
first placing run.**
Location: `updatebook/SKILL.md:281`.

The line counts every slide reference in the book (`build-book.py:4415-4423`). A second run, or a
book that already carried slides, prints the running total, and a slide placed twice counts
twice. Suggest: "every slide placed so far, in this run and earlier ones".

**6. Apply the slide-folder exclusion to the README record too.**
Location: `makebook/SKILL.md:104`, and `createbook/SKILL.md:701`.

The chapter side excludes the slide folder, but the README side takes "every figure the old
README records as placed". Picture an old README written before `slide_figures` existed that
lists slides as placed. Those slides become figure candidates, and the gate's "only one record
holds" list names each as a figure an edit removed. The README half was on `main` already, so
this is not a regression. The fix is one clause: "records as placed, outside the slide folder".

### Questions

1. **Finding 2.** Should slide notes added in a placing run be exempt from "several at once"? Or
   should a chapter that needs several be rewritten with its slides handed to the chapter agent?
   The first keeps "drafting places no slide". The second gets notes drafted with the slides in
   view, which is the option `NOTES.md` weighed and did not choose.
2. **Finding 1.** Was "after the paragraph it supports" meant to copy the old book's order: the
   point, then the slide, then the notes? A recreate carries no old notes, so in a recreated
   chapter the paragraph after a slide will rarely be its notes.
