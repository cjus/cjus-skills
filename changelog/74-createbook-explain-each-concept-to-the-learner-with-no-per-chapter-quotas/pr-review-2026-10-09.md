## PR review: createbook: explain each concept to the learner, with no per-chapter quotas

Reviewed: draft PR #75 for ticket #74, one commit (`2463162`) ahead of `main`. First review on this
branch; no earlier `pr-review-*.md` exists in this folder.

### Summary

The PR rewrites `/createbook`'s rule files so that a chapter explains each concept to its reader
rather than directing an explainer. It removes the per-chapter quotas on wrong models, analogies,
numbers, hedges and the simplification flag, and turns six new terms into a report. It also adds
a reader knowledge boundary, a coverage ledger and a sample stop to the outline workflow, and
records the method's origin in `NOTES.md`. Phase 10, the blind comparison, is pending by design
and is not assessed here.

### What is working well

- **Objective items 1 to 9 are each delivered, and each reaches every layer that needs it.** The
  four steps appear as a rule (`chapter-prose.md` § Explain each concept in four steps), in the
  plan ("Write the why in a line of its own"), in Before sending ("A concept with only an example
  there is the defect") and in the cut order ("Never cut a concept's why"). A rule stated once and
  never checked is the failure the core's own Before sending section exists to prevent, and this
  PR avoids it.
- **The operator's four rulings are carried out faithfully.** Six terms is now a report in the
  core, the outline (`SKILL.md` § 2), the gate (§ 3), the agent prompt (§ 5 item 12) and
  `updatebook/SKILL.md`. `narration.md` keeps its single required wrong model, and the core's
  "The profile file says whether a chapter needs one at all" still routes to it. Likely questions
  stay in the prose, and `guide.md` § Callouts keeps four labels.
- **The quotas are removed without dropping the forms they protected.** § Explaining devices have
  no quota keeps "never two for one idea", "A number is never bare" and the per-point hedge. That
  is the right reading of `NOTES.md` § What the numbers rest on: the count goes, and the
  per-instance rule stays.
- **"A fact is stated, not explained"** has a crisp test: "whether the reader could get it wrong by
  understanding it wrongly". It stops the four steps from inflating dates and room numbers, the
  over-application an agent would otherwise make.
- **The commentary on the first pair anticipates the template failure.** It says the rewrite
  "names none of the steps" and that a step name repeated in every chapter reads as a template,
  which `check-book.sh`'s cross-chapter phrase report would catch.
- **NOTES.md separates measured from asserted honestly.** "That is a case, and no figure in it is a
  rate" is the right claim for one reader, one book and three days.
- **Mechanical checks pass.** `scripts/check-citations.py` exits 0 with 203 resolved. Every bare
  in-file `§` reference the diff adds also resolves (checked by hand, since the script only checks
  backticked `file.md § X` forms). No em dash appears in `chapter-prose.md` or `guide.md`. The
  longest paragraph is 89 words (`chapter-prose.md:43`) and the longest sentence is exactly 45
  (`chapter-prose.md:330`, the cut order), at the limit and not over it. The cross-project audit
  of the changed files is clean, and CI passes on both legs.

### Facts in the before-and-after examples

All three pairs are factually sound.

- **Index, `chapter-prose.md:45-56`.** The Before's numbers are plausible. With about 67 rows per
  8 KB page, 40,000 rows fill about 600 pages. A B-tree on 40,000 integer keys is about two
  levels, so root, leaf and heap page come to three page reads. The After is correct: without an
  index, a lookup on that column is a full scan whose cost grows with the table.
- **git revert, `chapter-prose.md:64-73`.** Correct. `git revert` adds an inverse commit and
  leaves the original in history. After a force-pushed deletion, a teammate's `git pull` either
  stops or merges the deleted commit back. It stops on Git 2.33.1 and later with no `pull.rebase`
  or `pull.ff` set ("Need to specify how to reconcile divergent branches"). It merges the commit
  back under `pull.rebase=false`. "Can" correctly hedges the other configurations.
- **Full scan, `guide.md:201-211`.** Correct and hedged ("usually"). Small tables favour a
  sequential scan, and a scan for a selective query on a large table is the classic missing-index
  signal.

#### Important

🟡 **guide.md's paraphrase of what a chapter may assume now contradicts the core, and under the
guide profile it wins**

📍 Location: `plugins/bookcraft/skills/createbook/reference/guide.md:15`

**What I see:**

> What a chapter may assume is still set by `chapter-prose.md § What the reader arrives with`: a
> term an earlier chapter glossed is free, and anything else is glossed here.

The PR changed the core (`chapter-prose.md:19`) to grant "the fluent side of the reader's
knowledge boundary", and added `chapter-prose.md:23`: "Use anything on the fluent side plainly,
with no gloss." It left this paraphrase as it was. On `main` the two agreed; now they do not.

**The risk:**

`guide.md:3` says that where it names a rule in `chapter-prose.md`, `guide.md` wins, and this
sentence names one. A guide chapter agent therefore has an explicit instruction to gloss
everything an earlier chapter did not, fluent side included. For a reader who programs, that
means glossing "a function" or "a table". This is the default profile and the one the ticket's
evidence came from. It undercuts objective item 4 exactly where it matters most. It is not a
regression against `main`, where everything was glossed anyway.

**Suggested fix:**

```text
What a chapter may assume is still set by `chapter-prose.md § What the reader arrives with`:
the fluent side of the reader's knowledge boundary and a term an earlier chapter glossed are
free, and anything else is glossed here.
```

**Learning note:**

When a rule moves in the core, grep the profile files for every place that restates it, not
only the places that cite it by name. A paraphrase in a file with precedence is a fork of the
rule. The precedence clause makes the stale fork the one that wins.

🟡 **The sample part is drafted before `book.json` exists, then kept verbatim and copied as the
register**

📍 Location: `plugins/bookcraft/skills/createbook/SKILL.md:403-405` (the sample stop), which
precedes `SKILL.md:407` (§ 4. Write `book.json`)

**What I see:**

The sample is run "as a chapter agent with the § 5 prompt" at the end of § 3. The source keys
the marks must use, and the reader-facing `display` names the prose must use, are first settled
at § 4. Two places say so: `SKILL.md:301` ("Every `Yes` row becomes an entry in `book.json`'s
`sources` map at step 4, keyed by the shorthand the marks will use") and § 4's "in the same pass
that fixes the citation spellings". Then § 5 item 14 tells the sample's chapter agent to keep
"the approved part as it stands", and every other agent to match its register.

**The risk:**

The approved sample can carry mark keys that do not match `book.json` and cite a source by repo
path where `guide.md` § Provenance in prose requires the display name. Its own chapter inherits
both verbatim, and every other agent copies its register, including how it names sources. Step
7's `check-book.sh` and `check-provenance.sh` would catch the result. They would catch it after
the fan-out, which is the rewrite-every-chapter cost the sample stop exists to avoid.

**Suggested fix:**

§ 4 needs no operator input, so the cheapest fix is ordering. Open § 5 with the two sample
paragraphs, or move them to the end of § 4, and change the lead to "Once `book.json` is written
(§ 4), draft one sample part...". Then reword `SKILL.md:398` ("the first of the skill's two
blocking stops") so it still describes the sequence correctly.

**Learning note:**

A gate that approves an artifact "as it stands" freezes every input that artifact had at that
moment. Put it after the last step that fixes those inputs.

🟡 **"The question after that belongs in the concept list" conflicts with how the concept list
is fixed**

📍 Location: `plugins/bookcraft/skills/createbook/reference/chapter-prose.md:62` and its
commentary at `:75`. It conflicts with `chapter-prose.md:204-205`, `SKILL.md:307` and `SKILL.md`
§ 5 item 11.

**What I see:**

> Go one level past what the book requires the reader to do, and stop there: the question after
> that belongs in the concept list (§ Suggested reading).

§ Suggested reading says the items are "the gaps this chapter declined to fill, **which the
outline's source ledger names**", three to six of them. § 5 item 11 hands each agent "The concept
list this chapter ends on, from the ledger". `SKILL.md:307` gives the reason: writing the gaps
in the outline "is what stops twenty chapters each inventing their own edge".

**The risk:**

An agent following the new rule adds items the ledger never named. Each chapter then sets its
own edge, the thing the outline's gap list exists to prevent. It may also push a list past six,
or name something a later chapter covers. An agent that follows the ledger instead has nowhere
to put the question the new rule told it to place.

**Suggested fix:**

```text
Go one level past what the book requires the reader to do, and stop there. The question after
that is past the book's edge: where the outline's concept list for this chapter names it, the
reader finds it there, and where it does not, report it to the outline as a gap.
```

Adjust the git-revert commentary at `:75` to match.

**Learning note:**

A new rule that writes into an artifact another rule owns has to defer to that owner, or it
becomes a second, competing source of truth.

#### Suggestions

🟢 **Leftover budget wording around six terms, and a stale "lesson-script" label.** Four
phrases still frame six terms as an allowance:

- `SKILL.md:486` (§ 5 item 4): "without counting against this chapter's six"
- `SKILL.md:506`: "the chapter's term budget"
- `SKILL.md:344`: "at no cost against its six"
- `narration.md:11`: "costs nothing against the six new terms"

Each is still coherent with six as a report threshold, so none is wrong. "Term budget" and
"this chapter's six" are the phrasings most likely to lead an agent to economise on glosses.
Suggest "without counting toward the six past which this chapter reports itself too wide" and
"the chapter's terms". Leave `narration.md` alone, since `NOTES.md` records that its text is
deliberately unrewritten.

Separately, `SKILL.md:442`'s `slide_figures` row says "lesson-script slide art", a concept the
guide shape no longer has. Suggest "slide art the book uses as teaching aids". `makebook`'s
heading and the binder's comments can keep the name.

🟢 **The index pair models deleting the example, where the rule's first choice is keeping it.**
`chapter-prose.md:43` says "Give the example and then the reason, or the reason alone". The
After drops the 40,000-student figure entirely. `NOTES.md` notes that a drafting agent copies an
example more reliably than it meets a count, so this pair will teach agents to remove concrete
cases, the opposite of what the ticket's finding called for. An After that keeps "On a table of
40,000 students..." and then adds the sorted-copy reason models the preferred path. A minor
related point: the After's "keeps one column sorted" is a simplification with no hedge clause,
which the same file now requires at every simplification ("in the common kind of index" would
do).

🟢 **The explainer search terms will hit legitimate procedure steps.** `chapter-prose.md:305`
says to search for "ask the class" and "make sure they", and that "Each hit is an explanation
still to be written". A guide's numbered procedure for running a session (`guide.md` §
Procedures) can rightly say "Ask the class to submit before..." Suggest: "Read each hit: a line
telling an explainer what to say or land is an explanation still to be written, and a step the
reader performs stays."

🟢 **Two small accuracy points in NOTES.md.**

- `NOTES.md:774` says "The order is `qe`'s: the core idea, the mechanism, why it exists, and the
  failure it prevents". `qe`'s rule (`plugins/explain/skills/qe/SKILL.md`, the Style line)
  orders three steps. It names "the actual failure mode it prevents" only as a device preferred
  over an analogy. `NOTES.md:1088` describes this correctly. Suggest "The first three steps are
  `qe`'s order; the fourth came from the companion document."
- `NOTES.md:1134` counts "two wrong claims surfaced", but the ticket says "including a grading
  claim and a simulator", so two is a lower bound. The core's own rule is that a number keeps the
  scope it was measured over. Write "at least two".

🟢 **The README claims nothing rations wrong models.** `README.md:172` says "Nothing rations the
analogies, numbers, wrong models or hedges an explanation needs", but `narration.md:19` still
requires exactly one. Suggest "Nothing rations the analogies, numbers or hedges an explanation
needs."

🟢 **Coverage items have no recorded origin when nothing states them.** The coverage ledger's
"Stated in" column (`SKILL.md:315`) accepts a ledger source or the brief. Consider an argument
that names a reader, such as the README's own "for first-year CS students". There, § 1's ask
never fires and no course supplies graded work, so the items are the drafting agent's inference
with nothing to cite. The gate already handles the same case for the persona ("Say which part you
supplied"). Allowing `inferred` in "Stated in", and saying so at § 3, closes the gap.

🟢 **`/updatebook` does not carry the coverage ledger.** Its carry list (`updatebook/SKILL.md:121-130`)
and rewrite step 2 (`:233`) name the brief, terms, anchors and Carries row, but not the new
Teaches row. An edit that moves where a graded item is taught leaves the ledger stale. Adding
"the coverage ledger's Teaches row, when what the chapter teaches moved" is one bullet.
