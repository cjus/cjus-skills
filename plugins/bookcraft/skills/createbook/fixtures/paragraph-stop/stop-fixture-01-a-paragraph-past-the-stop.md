## In short

The only chapter of the paragraph-stop fixture covers the one stop the checker counts exactly:
a paragraph of more than 90 words.

[1-1] A paragraph is one idea, and the spec gives it a hard stop at 90 words.

## What grows a paragraph

[1-2] Revisions grow paragraphs, because adding a sentence to an existing paragraph renumbers
nothing and adding a new paragraph used to renumber everything after it. So each revision took
the cheap path, one more clause here and one more sentence there, and a paragraph that was 81
words at its first bind is 150 words two revisions later. Nothing counted it, so nothing stopped
it. The reader met a wall of text in the one place the chapter most needed to be scanned, and
nobody who edited it ever saw the whole paragraph at once, because each edit was small and
local and read fine on its own.

## What the checker does

[1-3] It counts every prose paragraph and names each one past the stop by its tag.
